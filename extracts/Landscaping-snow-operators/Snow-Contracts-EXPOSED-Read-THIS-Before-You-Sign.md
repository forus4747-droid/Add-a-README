## Cash flow traps — the core of this episode
An owner walks line by line through a **national snow-management broker's subcontractor agreement**, naming the clauses that cost him money.

- **Real case: $3,900 owed from January–March 2025, still unpaid in August.** With late fees it reached ~$5,400. Seven to eight months of carry on a small account.
- His economics on this work: **10–20% margin on sites worth $1,250 to $10,000**, against a **$60–70,000 truck**, a **$50,000/year salaried worker**, and **$10–20K snow blowers sitting on standby.** "It's not worth the headache."
- Expected re-signing probability on that contract as written: **"about 10%."**

**The clauses to hunt for (what it says → what he proposes instead):**
1. **Pay-when-paid.** Payment is contingent on the national receiving payment from *their* client. "If A doesn't pay B, then B doesn't pay C." → *"Payment shall not be contingent upon [national] receiving payment from its client. Subcontractor shall be paid within 30 days of invoice submission regardless of customer payment."* He concedes this is often the deal-breaker clause — push anyway.
2. **Real-time reporting forfeiture.** Failure to report service in real time, or invoice within 30 days, voids payment entirely. → *5-day notice to cure before payment can be denied; minor technical errors should not forfeit payment for valid services rendered;* resubmission allowed within 72 hours.
3. **All damages, whatever the cause.** "You're responsible for all damages, you shall repair and replace at your cost." → *liability limited to damage directly caused by negligent performance; claims reported within 5 days.* His example: someone smashes a handicap pylon at 5am after you plowed at 3am, and the 8am store manager bills you.
4. **First-service failure voids everything after.** Miss the first service and no future service gets paid — plus they back-charge you for a replacement contractor at inflated cost. → *pro-rate missed services, don't void the whole contract.* The trap is definitional: they wanted service at 2 inches, you arrived at 4, so what they call two missed runs you call one.
5. **All fines, including those assessed to the customer.** → *only fines arising directly from your own verified actions or omissions.* Snow pushed from the neighbouring property gets the building fined; it rolls down to you.
6. **One-way indemnification** — your insurance must be primary and non-contributory and cover the broker and its clients. → *mutual indemnification; not responsible for the gross negligence or wilful misconduct of the company or its clients.*
7. **Sole discretion to prorate, deny or reduce.** → *written notice of any reduction and a right to dispute within 10 business days.*

## What sellers hide — the structural point
> "They are counting on a certain amount of attrition. They're betting that 20% of us don't use the app, 20% don't take real-time photos or submit invoices in real time. Somebody has run the numbers saying we're going to do $30 million this year and we know we're going to have $10 million right to the bottom line because people aren't going to report correctly."

The compliance clauses are **a revenue line for the broker**, not an oversight. Also: he was once fully compliant, produced every geotag, invoice and photo from his software, got paid — **and was still de-rated as a contractor.**

**Direct application to buying:** a snow or landscaping business with revenue subcontracted from nationals or property-management brokers has (a) receivables that are much slower than the AR ageing suggests, (b) contingent back-charge exposure, and (c) no direct customer relationship — the contract is with the broker, not the site. Ask to read every subcontractor agreement and check for pay-when-paid before valuing that revenue at all.

## Winning work
- You will not always get to use your own paper: "you don't always get that opportunity as you sub out for the larger property management companies." But **"you don't have to sign anything without having a conversation"** — ask to red-line.
- His workaround for not having a $5–20K legal retainer: **run the whole contract through ChatGPT** ($20/month) to surface red flags and suggest protective additions. He is explicit it is not legal advice and you should still use a lawyer where you can.
- The relationship test he puts to counterparties: "Are you looking for a gotcha, or do you want to partner with me on this site for the next 10 years?"
