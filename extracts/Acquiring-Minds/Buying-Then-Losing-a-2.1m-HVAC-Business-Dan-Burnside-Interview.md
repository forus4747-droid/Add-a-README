# Buying Then Losing a $2.1m HVAC Business — Dan Burnside

- **Channel:** Acquiring Minds · **Track:** A · **Duration:** 1:46:11
- **URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9S129U7rVV0
- **tags:** HVAC, due-diligence, customer-concentration, key-man-risk, SBA, rural, deal-failure, add-backs, under-the-table

## Key numbers
- Purchase price **$2.1M** on **$2.1M revenue / $800K EBITDA** — a multiple **under 3x**. "On paper the deal was amazing."
- Buyer funded **10%**; seller took **full cash payout, no seller note, no rolled equity**. Seller retained the building and leased it back.
- **$400K revolver** drawn down over the period; ~**$115K** working capital at close.
- **15% of revenue** came from plumbing/water-heater work that was **illegal without a licence** — discovered after close.
- The departing refrigeration tech took **~1/3 of the business's profits** with him (high-margin refrigeration + the hospital account).
- Under-the-table cash wages to staff represented **15–25% of the reported EBITDA**.
- Net: **~55% of stated EBITDA gone on day one**, before operating anything. SBA debt service alone typically runs **~50% of EBITDA**.
- 12 employees at close; buyer's own screen was "no business under 10 employees" on the assumption **~half of staff churn** post-close.
- 7-month search, **4 LOIs** — outbid twice, walked from two in diligence.

## What to look for
**Red flags, all of them present here and all findable before close:**
- Seller "always bought inventory at year end to lower the tax burden" (the *other* deal he walked from) — a recurring purchase dressed up as a one-off add-back.
- Revenue that requires a **licence the company does not hold**. Ask which trades/permits each revenue line depends on, and whether a change of control voids them.
- **No CRM / paper records** — meant he could not trace which customers belonged to which technician. That blindness is what let a third of profits walk.
- An employee who "only works a couple of days a week" — here, because the owner had been **letting him siphon customers for years** (Sonic, McDonald's) to build his own competing HVAC firm.
- Owner's son on staff, described by the seller as uninterested in running it — in fact he resented the new owner and, as one of only two refrigeration techs, immediately demanded a **25% raise**.
- Cash wages under the table: inflates EBITDA *and* leaves you unable to match take-home pay legally. Staff resented the "raise" because the taxed bonus was less than the old cash.
- Seller reneged on the agreed **3-month transition down to 2 weeks, one week before closing** — and the buyer had already quit, sold his house and moved.
- **Green flag that misled:** immaculate shop, strong personal rapport (both Eagle Scouts, both wrestlers), a straight-talking broker. Rapport is a sales technique, not diligence.

**Structural lesson — the "Chuck in a truck" problem:** in trades, any competent technician can start competing tomorrow with no debt service and no overhead. Within a week of close the market went from 2 competitors to 6, and they could underbid below his cost to operate. Buying in a tertiary market to avoid private equity put him against something worse: competitors with nothing to service and lifelong local relationships.

## Canadian-specific
US deal (SBA financing, Colorado licensing). **US — verify for Canada.** Canadian read-across:
- The SBA 10%-down full-cash-out structure has no direct Canadian twin; CSBFP is capped far lower and Canadian acquisition deals lean much harder on **vendor take-backs**. Burnside's own retrospective — "if I could have held something over him, that would have been really helpful" — is the single best argument for insisting on a VTB.
- Trade licensing is provincial in Canada; the same question applies (does the target hold the licence for every revenue line, and does it survive a share vs asset sale?).

## Verdict
**Worth the read — one of the highest-value items in the corpus.** A rare honest post-mortem with the numbers attached. The value is not "HVAC is bad" but the anatomy of how a sub-3x multiple on $800K EBITDA becomes zero earnings before you have made a single decision. Read it beside any listing that looks cheap. Also the best available argument for a seller note and for buying where you can actually live.
