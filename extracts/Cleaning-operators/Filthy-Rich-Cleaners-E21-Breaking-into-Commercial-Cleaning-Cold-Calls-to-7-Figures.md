## Unit economics from the owner's mouth
- Father-in-law's company had shrunk to **$600K**; changing only the sales process took it to **$1.8M in about 2 years.** 100% commercial, office buildings.
- Original niche screen: **office buildings above 25,000 sq ft** — "they just wanted one full-time person at minimum working that location." Anything smaller was declined.
- Later niche (churches and private schools): **average school ≈ $8,000/month.** "If you have 250 kids in there, you're looking at five to eight thousand dollars a month."
- **Production rate used: 3,000 sq ft per hour per person** for school facilities, including mopping and dust mopping. A 24,000 sq ft school = 8 hours = one full-time person or two part-timers.
- SEO became the top channel by exit: **90–95% of new business came from web search**, on spend of ~$1,500–2,000/month. Acquired the business 2011, **exited 2022**.

## Winning work — the estimating method
**Never bid on square footage alone.** "If you bid a 10,000 sq ft office and it's just enclosed offices, that takes a lot quicker to clean than 10,000 sq ft in cubicles." A 10,000 sq ft construction office versus a surgical centre — same area, wildly different time.

**Four ways he estimates time:**
1. **Experience** — after 1,000+ walkthroughs he can price anything ≤50,000 sq ft on sight.
2. **Production rate** — 3,000 sq ft/hour/person, adjusted for the facility.
3. **Count method** — 10 classrooms × 10 minutes = 100 minutes; then **the ISSA "3x rule" for bathrooms: add toilets + urinals + sinks, multiply by 3 for minutes.** A bathroom with 8 toilets, 2 urinals and 2 sinks = 12 × 3 = **36 minutes**. Common areas run **a quarter to a third of the whole facility**.
4. **Comparison** — price against an existing account with the same room count.

**Walkthrough process (worth copying verbatim):**
- Research first: Google Earth for size, social media photos for dust in vents and other pain points. **Shrubs or trees adjacent to the building = they have a bug problem inside** (entry points at the windows). Identify the decision-makers; if more than one person shows up, ask point blank whether there are others you should talk to.
- Show up **5 minutes early with a clean car** — inside as well as out, "they'll walk you to the car after."
- Two minutes of pleasantries, then **"Can you show me around?"** — always the first question.
- A dozen questions, half to surface pain, half logistical: *What do you like best about your current cleaning service? What do you like least? If we could change one thing tomorrow, what would it be?* (They always give different answers.) Then: how many days, earliest evening start, and **who supplies toilet paper, paper towels, hand soap, trash liners** — which changes the price.
- Close by **summarising back for 2 minutes** ("servers who repeat the order get more tips"), then a **30-second elevator pitch — the only selling in the whole visit** — then next steps and leave.

**Two hard rules:**
- **Never give a price during the walkthrough.** "As soon as you announce your price, they're going to judge you on the price for the rest of that walkthrough. They're not going to listen to a word you say." And shooting from the hip is how you underbid.
- **Never criticise the existing cleaning.** He lost a bid by saying "this cleaning company really did a bad job on these floors" — the facilities manager replied *"I did those floors."* Often it's the manager, a friend, a wife, or an employee's daughter.
- He also refuses laser measuring on site: "you're there taking their time, being a disruption — that's a friction point."

## Niche selection
- He counted ~**30 viable niches**: churches, schools, banks, gyms, dance studios, government, office buildings, office suites, industrial, agricultural. **Pick one.**
- Why he left office buildings: "you're bidding against 10 other people, 20 other companies." His partner used to **make cow noises under his breath** as they were herded through group walkthroughs. "There's no relationship building whatsoever in those types of facilities."
- Churches and private schools: **only one or two other bidders**, and some of those never submit. Churches are easy to clean and need the facility cleaned all week; private schools run summer camps so it is **year-round revenue**. Note: US *public* school districts largely do not outsource — he targeted private, Catholic, Lutheran, Episcopalian schools.
- On winning without a track record: "Ever see The Wizard of Oz? He's just this small little guy who makes himself look real big." Good website, branded email not Gmail, address their pain points, right price point.
- Free tooling tip: prompt ChatGPT as "you are a marketing expert targeting [niche], give me five pain points they may have around their commercial cleaning service" to learn a niche before you have worked in it.

## What sellers hide / cautions
- His own first bid, to a church, **omitted labour taxes, supplies and any net profit line** — and they signed instantly. A signed contract proves nothing about whether it is priced correctly. When you buy a book of business, re-price every contract from scratch.
- The one-off big job: a peer took a refugee-housing contract and insisted on **hourly rather than a day rate** — it became a $1M+ job and the scope changed constantly. "You have no idea how grateful I am I did hourly."

## Canadian-specific
US operator (Washington DC area). **US — verify for Canada.** The ISSA 3x bathroom rule, the 3,000 sq ft/hour production rate and the entire walkthrough script are jurisdiction-neutral. The niche logic transfers directly: in Canadian markets, private schools, churches, and independent industrial shops are similarly under-bid compared with downtown office towers.
