## Unit economics from the owner's mouth
**Cost structure (Queen Bee Cleaning, ~22–25 employees):**
- **Labour ≈ 60% of revenue** — "our most expensive thing is the labour… we have to pay good wages otherwise we don't keep good people."
- **Supplies ≈ 10%. Marketing + operations ≈ 10%. Net ≈ 15–20%.**
- Cleaners paid **$22–24/hr**, best/longest-tenured **$25/hr**.
- Revenue: **$60K/month last year → $120K/month now**, "and sometimes we turn jobs away."

**Second operator (Spruce Cleaning):** **$100–115K/month revenue**, claims **45% margin** ("one of the highest in our industry" — treat sceptically against the 15–25% others report). Growth 18–25% month over month.

**Third operator (MaidThis, remote/franchised):** **$100–150K/month for the LA location alone**, **25% net margin**; states industry standard is **12–30%** for a remote-local model. 10 years in, run in a few hours a week.

**Airbnb turnover economics (the most specific numbers in the corpus):**
- Charge **$400–500 per turnover** all-in (clean + laundry + supplies). Minimum ticket **$250**; the villa filmed was **$380**.
- **3 cleaners per unit, 3–4 hours** on a 3–4 bed / 2–3 bath.
- Worked cost: **~$50 labour + ~$30 supplies + $20–30 parking ≈ $100**, against ~$350 charged.
- Net **~20% ≈ $80 per job**; ~**18 jobs** in the period discussed.
- The real margin is not cleaning: **co-hosting at 10–20% of the unit's gross revenue.** "If you offer only cleaning, the margins are very small."
- Airbnb beats occupied homes on frequency: an occupied home is once or twice a month; an Airbnb in high season is **four to five times a week, back to back**.

**Startup costs:** one started with **under $1,000** (WordPress template ~$100, logo made in Microsoft Word, name taken from Reddit). Another with **$5,000**: $4,000 for a 2003 Honda Accord, $500 backpack vacuum, $300 supplies, remainder on flyers and business cards.

## Winning work
- **Answering the phone is the moat.** "If you try to call the top 10 competitors in your city for cleaning, seven of them won't even pick up."
- Hard number: pick up **within the first couple of rings → 50–60% close rate.** Call back a few minutes later → **16.5%.** Missed calls get an automatic AI text.
- Calls are routed to a **remote team in South Africa** working the local time zone.
- **Marketing spend:** Google Ads ~$4,000/mo (Google Local Services — "these customers are ready to buy"), Yelp ~$750, Facebook ~$1,000. A different operator runs **Yelp only at $2,900/month producing $110–150K/month**, claiming $35K returned per $1K spent.
- **Budget rule:** mature business ~**5% of revenue** on marketing; early stage **15–20%** — because it is recurring revenue, "overspend that first year to scale up, then level it down."
- **Capturing demand vs creating demand:** Google/Yelp/Thumbtack capture people already searching; flyers, postcards and brownies to past customers create it. Flyer the neighbours of every house you already clean — five rounds — because you already know the demographic fits.
- Pricing discovery method: **call three competitors pretending to be a customer** and ask for a quote on a 3-bed/2-bath. One owner's first job was a move-out quoted at **$90 that took two people 12 hours.**
- Craigslist trick: **publish your prices in the ad** to filter out anyone unwilling to pay them.

## Staffing reality
- Hiring is industrialised: a dedicated person posts jobs, screens, interviews and onboards; separate cleaners are dedicated to training. "It's like an assembly line — you're cleaning by yourself in a week." **20 cleaners added in two months.**
- Quality control on Airbnb work is photographic: document damage *before* cleaning starts, photograph after, or you eat the re-clean for free.

## What sellers hide / cautions
- The 45%-margin claim sits against 15–25% from the other two operators in the same video. **Ask which line items are excluded** — owner labour, marketing, parking, re-cleans.
- Marketing dependency is a concentration risk in disguise: an operator doing $110–150K/month from **Yelp alone** has one channel between him and zero.
- The "$1,000 to start" framing ignores the real cost, which the owner names himself: "hundreds of hours researching, trial and error… It's not the money, it's the time spent."

## Canadian-specific
US operators (LA, Seattle). **US — verify for Canada.** Wage figures ($22–25/hr) are broadly comparable to urban Canadian cleaner wages; Google Local Services Ads operate in Canada, Yelp is materially weaker here, so the Yelp-only model does not transfer. Airbnb turnover economics depend on local short-term-rental rules, which in BC and several Ontario municipalities are far more restrictive than in the US markets shown — **check municipal STR bylaws before valuing an Airbnb-heavy client book.**

## Verdict
**Worth the read — the densest source of cleaning unit economics in the corpus.** Discount the promotional framing and the 45% margin claim, but the labour-60/supplies-10/marketing-10/net-15-20 split, the wage range, the Airbnb per-turnover build-up and the answered-call close-rate numbers are all concrete and directly usable when reading a listing or interrogating a seller.
