## Key numbers
The listing: commercial janitorial in central Michigan, **founded 1965 (65 years old)**.
- **2022 revenue $1.3M · SDE $213K** (~16% SDE margin).
- **23 W-2 employees + 25 W-9 independent contractors.**
- Two leased buildings, ~2,200 sq ft each, 680 sq ft of office. No asking price given.
- Services: floor care, emergency cleanup, construction site cleanup, window cleaning, plus a wholesale cleaning-supply distribution arm. Customers: local government, universities, healthcare, state affiliates.

**The debt-service math they run on air (the most useful part of the episode):**
- At a **4x multiple → $852K price**. 20% down (~$170K), financing ~**$680K over a 7-year amortisation at 6%** → **annual debt service ~$122K**.
- $213K SDE − $122K debt service = **~$90K/year left**, which is *both* your salary and your entire margin of safety.
- "If one client cancels you go from making 90 grand a year to 60 grand a year" — one $2,500/month customer.
- Conclusion: **it doesn't trade at 4x.** Reverse-engineer the price from what a lender will allow at a **1.25–1.5x debt service coverage ratio**.

## What to look for
**The three claims on SDE** — the framework worth memorising:
> SDE is not all yours. It divides into three pies: **(1) debt service — paid first, it is not in the tipping order; (2) the buffer before decline hits your pocket; (3) the owner's comp — last in line.**
On a $3M-EBITDA business a lot can go wrong before you're on the street. On a $213K-SDE business "you start absorbing the go-wrong stuff right out of your personal cash flow almost immediately."

**Green flags:**
- **Stickiness.** B2B cleaning is "the type of thing you just don't cancel for 20 years… all you've got to do is clear the bar of did you mop the floor and did you do it on time." B2B buyers are far less price-sensitive than residential — the host tells two stories of keeping vendors he actively disliked purely to avoid re-running a purchase process.
- **Age as signal (Lindy).** 65 years of survival tells you something real about the brand and the service.
- **Differentiation is the whole game:** data centres (specialised equipment, big premium), biology labs, secure/cleared facilities, and industrial plant-shutdown cleaning (20 people for a two-week window while the plant loses millions a day) all command real pricing power.

**Red flags:**
- **Why is it not bigger after 65 years?** With genuinely sticky clients, adding one a year should have compounded. That gap is a diligence item — it implies churn.
- **Scope drift.** The wholesale supply distribution arm reads as distraction, not diversification: "go grow the high margin service instead of the low margin distribution."
- **Small-client base risk:** if clients are 5,000 sq ft offices you get "ankle bitten by all of these guys with a vacuum who underbid the contract by 40% because they have no overhead, don't know how to price it, and aren't valuing their own time." Barriers to entry are ~$50–100 of equipment. Million-sq-ft buildings needing a team of 40 a night are a different and much better business.
- **The 1099 half of the workforce.** Verify IRS-compliant treatment — not directing their activities, etc. — or they can later sue for benefits and W-2 status.
- **"Customary and reasonable transition assistance"** in a listing is entirely negotiable: "could be two weeks or six months, you don't really know."

## Track B crossover — staffing reality
- Clients want the work done **nights and weekends**, so you are staffing third-shift, part-time, often **second-job employees**. "As soon as it hits the fan, which job are they not going to show up for? Yours."
- "You're pinning your whole reputation on the guy with the second side hustle."
- The fix is systems, not heroics: **GPS check-in within 15 minutes of scheduled time, automatic alert to a dispatcher (not the owner), a call at 15 minutes, a standby crew dispatched at 30** — plus photo proof of completion. "It is not hard to be unimpeachable here as having done the job on time."
- Two viable models: a lead/helper model (W-2 lead holds keys and supplies, 1099 helpers) or captive crews plus fully self-sufficient subcontracted crews.
- Theft matters — you are putting people inside someone else's office unsupervised at night.

## Canadian-specific
US listing and US lending assumptions. **US — verify for Canada.** Two direct read-acrosss:
- The DSCR reverse-engineering logic is lender-agnostic and works identically with a Canadian bank or CSBFP: work out the maximum debt the cash flow supports, and that caps your price.
- The 1099/W-2 compliance question maps to the CRA's own contractor-vs-employee tests, and the exposure (retroactive source deductions, CPP/EI) is comparable.
- One of the hosts notes central Michigan's snow *helps* — winter drives more janitorial work and lets you cross-sell plowing and restriping. That argument applies with more force in Canada.

## Verdict
**Worth the read — the best single teardown of a small janitorial listing in the corpus.** Two things to take: the three-claims-on-SDE framework, and the conclusion that a $213K-SDE cleaning business is a job with debt attached unless you are bolting it onto existing density. Their own answer for when it *does* make sense — buying the client list as a bolt-on in the same or an adjacent geography — is precisely the strategy Boyden & Furfaro executed.
