# Run The Test

$39. Three weeks. A real yes or no.

## Schedule

| Days | Do |
|---|---|
| **1–3** | Buy domain, set up Workspace, start warming it. Pick ONE niche. |
| **4–7** | Build the 100-prospect list. Domain still warming — send nothing. |
| **8–9** | Produce 10 free samples for the worst-backlog prospects. |
| **10** | Send the 10 sample emails (Email 1A). |
| **11–17** | Send the other 90 (Email 1B), 20–30/day. |
| **14–21** | Follow-ups at +4 days each. Deliver samples to anyone who says yes. Close. |

## Kill criteria — write these down before you send email one

| Trigger | Action |
|---|---|
| 100 sent, **fewer than 2 replies** | Offer is wrong. Keep the niche, change the deliverable. |
| 200 sent, **0 calls or sample requests** | Niche is wrong. Keep the offer, change the niche. |
| 300 sent, **0 paid** | Kill this offer entirely. Restart from the offer document. |
| 3 offers × 300 sent, **0 paid** | Cold email is not your channel. The model is not disproven — the channel is. |
| **Any 1 paid client** | **Proven. Stop testing. Do it 10 more times.** |

The point of writing these down now is that in week three you will want to renegotiate
them. That is exactly when they are worth having.

## What "success" is

**One paying client.** Not ten. Not $10k. One person who sent you money for a thing you
delivered. That single event converts this from a theory into a business, and everything
after it is repetition.

## Expected numbers at benchmark

- 100 emails → 3–6 replies
- 3–6 replies → 1–3 sample requests
- 1–3 samples → 0–1 paying clients

**Read that last line honestly.** One round of 100 may produce zero. That is a normal
result, not a failed business — it is why the kill criteria go to 300 before you quit
the offer. Budget for three rounds.
