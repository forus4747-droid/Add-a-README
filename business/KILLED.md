# ⚠️ THIS OFFER IS INVALIDATED — DO NOT RUN IT

Everything in `00-OFFER.md` through `05-RUN-THE-TEST.md` was built before running a
competitor check. The check was run afterwards and the offer does not survive it.

## What killed it

**The deliverable is already a software feature, sold by funded companies, at my price.**

| Competitor | Price/mo | What it already does |
|---|---|---|
| **Podium** | $399 | "Review Response Agent" — context-aware on-brand AI replies, with automatic escalation for sensitive reviews |
| **Birdeye** | $299 | "Review Response Agent" — AI replies by tone and sentiment; can auto-respond by star rating without a human |
| **NiceJob** | $75 (+$199 setup) | Automated review *generation* — which was the proposed "moat" |

**Podium and Birdeye ship my entire deliverable as an automated feature.** NiceJob does
the review-generation pivot for a quarter of the proposed price.

**And the buyer is already spending there.** Medical, dental and legal practices pay
**$299–599/month** for reputation management as a category. The price point was right.
The category is occupied.

**Churn would be brutal.** Agencies with 1–10 staff run **25–32% annual churn**. A solo
operator selling a service whose software substitute is one search away would run worse.

**White-label resale is already commoditised** — Vendasta, Grade.us, EmbedMyReviews,
Climbo all exist, so the "agency" version of this play is a crowded reseller market.

## What genuinely remains — smaller than advertised

The software wins on capability and loses on adoption:

- Users report **"significant setup and learning time"**, trouble loading their customer
  lists, and widget problems
- Birdeye locks most deals into **12-month contracts** (no cancelling at month 6) and adds
  an **~8% "Innovation Fee"** at renewal
- Podium is **$399 for two locations**, $50/location after, with add-ons users describe as
  surprise billing
- Small businesses cancel over **cost and complexity**, not capability

So the real gap is **bought-but-never-configured software**, which is a services gap, not a
product gap. That is a genuine business — "we run the tool you're already paying for" — but
it is a smaller, lower-margin one than what was written up here, and it is *not* what these
documents describe.

## The procedural lesson

Question one for any service offer, asked **before** building anything:

> **Does software already do this, and what does it charge?**

If a funded company ships it as a feature at your price point, you are not entering a
market — you are entering a price war against a sales team.

None of this invalidates the productized-service *model* from `../proof/`. It invalidates
this specific offer.
