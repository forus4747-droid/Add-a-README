# The Emails

Benchmarks you are measuring against: **3.4%** average B2B reply rate, **5.8%** for lists
under 50, **>5%** is strong. Under 2% means something is wrong — see kill criteria.

Rules for all of them: plain text, no images, no logo, no signature block with six links,
no attachments on the first email. Send from your real domain. Send Tue–Thu, 7–9am local.

---

## EMAIL 1A — for the 10 free-sample prospects

Subject: `your last 12 reviews`

> Hi {FirstName},
>
> I noticed {Business} has {N} Google reviews with no reply — including a really
> good one from {ReviewerName} in {Month} about {specific thing they praised}.
>
> I drafted replies to your 12 most recent ones. They're here, no charge, use them
> or bin them: {link}
>
> I wrote them to sound like a practice, not a robot. Change the names if you like
> them but want them in your own words.
>
> If it's useful I do this as an ongoing thing — I clear the whole backlog and then
> handle every new review within 24 hours. Happy to send details, or happy to leave
> it here if not.
>
> {YourName}
> {Address} · unsubscribe: reply "stop"

**Why it works:** it is not a pitch. There is no ask in the first 80% of it. The
proof of competence arrives before the request, and costs them nothing to verify.

## EMAIL 1B — for the other 90

Subject: `{Business} — 23 unanswered`

> Hi {FirstName},
>
> {Business} has {N} Google reviews sitting without a reply, going back to {Month}.
>
> That matters more than it looks — Google ranks businesses that respond above ones
> that don't, and patients read the replies as much as the reviews. A practice that
> answers reads as one that's paying attention.
>
> I clear the whole backlog in the owner's voice, then handle every new review within
> 24 hours. {NearbyCompetitor} replies to all of theirs.
>
> Want me to draft your five most recent ones so you can see the quality? No charge,
> no catch — takes me twenty minutes.
>
> {YourName}
> {Address} · unsubscribe: reply "stop"

**The ask is a sample, not a call.** Asking for 20 minutes of your time converts far
better than asking for 20 minutes of theirs.

## EMAIL 2 — follow-up, 4 days later, same thread

Subject: `Re: {original subject}`

> Hi {FirstName} — sending this once more in case it got buried.
>
> Since I wrote, {Business} has picked up {X} more reviews. {ShortDetail}.
>
> The offer stands: I'll draft five replies free so you can judge the quality.
> If it's not a priority right now just say so and I'll stop emailing.
>
> {YourName}

**Then stop.** One follow-up. Two makes you the thing people complain about.

## Personalising 100 emails without dying

Only ONE line needs to be genuinely custom: the specific detail. Everything else is
merge fields. Paste your tracker rows into Claude:

```
Here are 20 businesses with their most recent Google review text.
For each, write ONE sentence I can drop into a cold email that proves
I actually looked at their profile. Reference something specific —
a reviewer's name, a service, a complaint pattern. No compliments
that could apply to any business. One sentence each, numbered.
```

## Sending mechanics

- **Warm the domain for 10–14 days first.** Send normal low-volume email from it before
  the campaign. Skipping this means you measure your spam score, not your offer.
- **20–30/day maximum**, sent manually from your inbox. No sending tool for the first 100.
- Track opens if you like, but **judge on replies only** — open rates are inflated garbage.
