I think packages in general are just kind of interesting. When you're thinking about packages, like the lifetime is fascinating, the 10 years is interesting, the five-year — how are you thinking about value stacking those? The easiest part about any service membership or warranty is it has to be super simple, and they have to be different at every package bundle level. One of the mistakes could be that everything's got a lifetime warranty no matter what you buy here. That's not going to create value, because if the option that costs 500 bucks has got a lifetime warranty and the one that costs $5,000 got a lifetime warranty, why am I inspired to do the better one when the coverage is the same?

Everything's the same, right. So when it comes to the value stack of, say, a service membership, I think people overcomplicate and make way too much out of it. Simply, you don't even need a piece of paper or anything that represents it. It's like: there's three things you're going to get. And the rule of creating a membership, what I've learned in the 47 years of doing this, is that it's got to be so simple that the dumbest guy in your company has got to be able to know it by heart.

That's the key thing, because if the customer asks that dumbest guy in the company "hey is that service membership any good" and he goes "I have no idea what you're talking about", you're going to be in trouble. Especially like you John, when you got a $20 million company. You got to say, does everybody in the company know what that thing is, clearly know the benefits, because they will be asked when they're delivering parts out there to somebody or picking up the garbage or something like that. They'll be asked by the customer: "hey, since you're the guy who delivers stuff, I got that service membership — is that really any good?" They're going to have an off-the-record kind of a conversation, or the installers, right. And if they don't have a positive response or understand completely...

Here's the three benefits. The three benefits are really this: you get the front-of-the-line pass, you go right to the front of the line, get to cut in front of all the other customers if you get this agreement. Number two, you get free diagnostic the rest of the year. And number three, you get any on-demand or scheduled update or maintenance on the system that we have — whatever system, electrical, plumbing — it could be either on demand for plumbing or it could be scheduled for HVAC, but that's included with it. The reason I include only those three things is because those are three things you can't lie about.

A discount, a 15% discount, I don't even believe in that because I believe it's a lie on face. Because what people do is they create the value rate price as the price they want, and then they raise the other price by 15% and then they call it a discount, which I don't really feel is ethical. I feel like if you're a premium company and trying to represent the premium solution in your marketplace, you don't offer discounts. If you're doing it that way, you just offer better service, and that's what people purchase. Nobody believes the discounts are real.

You get work at a place like Wilson or whatever, it's like "that's your discount, 18,000 is my discount price" — holy, you know. And then you're almost challenging people to look for a lower price. Like, there's our discount rate at 18,000, it normally costs 24,000. It's like, Jesus, really? I bet I can find somebody for less than 24,000.
