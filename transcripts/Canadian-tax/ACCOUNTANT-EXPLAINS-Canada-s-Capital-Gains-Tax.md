The 2024 budget was released by the government on April 16th 2024 and there were shocking changes that have caused a lot of confusion. In today's video I will be going over the tax changes for capital gains, tax breaks for entrepreneurs, and crypto reporting to the CRA. Capital gains tax. The capital gains inclusion rate is changing for the first time in over two decades. Canada's tax system has historically had a 50% inclusion rate when it comes to capital gains and losses since 2001.

The fact that the government is proposing to change the inclusion rate is going to have a domino effect and impact many parts of the current tax system. How capital gains tax works. There are three main ways income gets taxed in Canada: earned income, capital gains, and dividends. If you earn $70,000 in salary your taxes are about $17,000 in 2023. On the other hand, capital gains result from the sale of capital property such as real estate, stocks and crypto, and when you earn $70,000 in capital gains only 50% is subject to tax, so you would essentially be taxed on only $35,000 of capital gains and your taxes would only be $4,500 in 2023.

When you compare earned income like salary to capital gains, you are a lot more tax efficient earning income as capital gains as you are only taxed on half of it. With budget 2024, the government's intention is to increase the inclusion rate on capital gains realized annually above $250,000, effective June 25th 2024. The capital gains inclusion of 50% would not change up to the $250,000 threshold for individuals. Every dollar above the $250,000 threshold would be subject to the new proposed inclusion rate of 2/3 or 66.67%. So in most cases if you have capital gains under $250,000 the new proposed changes won't affect you.

Example: if you made $300,000 in capital gains and you are in the highest marginal tax bracket of 53.5%, before June 25th all your capital gains are subject to the 50% inclusion rate — 50% of $300,000 is $150,000, times 53.5% marginal rate, giving total taxes of $80,250. After June 25th there are two tiers: the first $250,000 still at 50% inclusion, the next $50,000 at 66.67%. The total taxes after June 25th would now be about $84,700 based on 2023 tax rates, which results in about $4,500 more in taxes. The projected number of Canadians affected is 0.13% of all people, with average gross income of $1.4 million, and it's not supposed to affect 99.87% of the Canadian population with an average gross income of $60,000. Note there will be no changes to the existing principal residence exemption, which means capital gains from the sale of your principal residence is 100% tax exempt.

Also any income including capital gains is tax sheltered in accounts like your RRSP, RRIF, TFSA, FHSA or RESP. Two main tax breaks for entrepreneurs. The first is the lifetime capital gains exemption, which means an individual can be exempt for capital gains realized on the sale of qualified small business corporation shares. Under budget 2024 this lifetime capital gains exemption will be increased to $1.25 million for sales happening on or after June 25th 2024. The second tax break is called the Canadian Entrepreneurs' Incentive.

This is brand new and proposed as part of budget 2024. In a nutshell, individuals may be eligible to only include one-third of capital gains on the sale of qualifying shares for an additional $2 million in their lifetime. However this $2 million won't be released all at once but will be phased in increments of $200,000 per year starting on January 1st 2024 and reaching a value of $2 million by January 1st 2034. This means individuals selling qualifying small business corporation shares can have up to $3.25 million in total exemption and partial exemption — the $1.25 million is the lifetime capital gains exemption so 100% tax-free, and then the $2 million is the Canadian Entrepreneurs' Incentive where the inclusion rate is only 33.33% instead of 66.67%. This way Canadian entrepreneurs are better off for capital gains up to the $6.25 million mark, and then any capital gains after $6.25 million will be taxed higher than the previous tax regime due to the higher inclusion rate of 66.67% instead of 50%.

Capital gains tax for corporations. With the new inclusion rate of 2/3 or 66.67%, this affects all capital gains earned by corporations and there is no $250,000 threshold like the one that exists for individuals. This would only affect about 12.6% of corporations that have capital gains income; the proposed changes shouldn't affect about 87.4% of corporations that have average taxable income of $174,000. With the changes to the capital gains inclusion rate this is going to throw off something called integration, where essentially the money that you take out from the corporation and then tax at the shareholder level should be the same as if the shareholder were to receive that income personally. Crypto asset reporting.

Budget 2024 also proposes the Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework with a new annual reporting requirement in the Income Tax Act. This would mean that any crypto asset service providers like crypto exchanges, brokers, dealers and even operators of teller machines would now be obligated to report to the CRA details of each customer.
