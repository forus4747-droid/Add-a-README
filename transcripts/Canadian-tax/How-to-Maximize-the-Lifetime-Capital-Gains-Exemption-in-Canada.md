The lifetime capital gains exemption can be one of the most valuable tools to help reduce tax bills. The lifetime capital gains exemption is a tax benefit that reduces the tax Canadian individuals must pay on capital gains from the sale of certain qualified assets. These qualified assets include the sale of qualified small business corporation shares, qualified farm property, and qualified fishing property. In this video we focus on the disposition of qualified small business corporation shares. In 2023, the lifetime capital gains exemption limit per individual on the disposal of qualified small business corporation shares is $971,190.

Since only 50% of capital gains is taxable, the actual exemption limit becomes $485,000 and change. This amount is adjusted each year to account for inflation. Here's an example. If you dispose of qualified small business corporation shares and earned a million dollars in capital gains, the lifetime capital gains exemption allows you to shelter a portion of the capital gains from taxation up to the specified limit of $971,190. You will only need to pay tax on $14,405 as opposed to $500,000.

The lifetime capital gains exemption has a lifetime cumulative limit, meaning you can claim the exemption whenever qualified assets are disposed until the limit is used up. For small business corporation shares to be qualified, the shares must be from a Canadian controlled private corporation, commonly known as a CCPC. These shares must be held for at least 24 months before the sale. Additionally, the corporation must meet specific criteria, such as being primarily engaged in active business carried on in Canada. The exemption can be used in various ways.

If you own shares of your business, or if your business shares are owned by your family trust and you're a beneficiary of the trust, you can use your lifetime capital gains exemption when you sell your business. For example, if you own 100% of the shares of your business and sold it for 2 million, close to 1 million would qualify for your lifetime capital gains exemption, meaning you would only pay tax on a little over 500,000. Depending on how your corporate structure is set up and how many family members own it, you may be able to take advantage of other family members' lifetime capital gains exemptions when selling your business. Another strategy is to spread out the lifetime capital gains exemption limit and claim it over time. This spreads out the taxable income over multiple years and maximizes the exemption available to you.

For example, you can sell your qualified small business shares over a period and slowly use up your lifetime capital gains exemption. We recently had a client who owned a million in stock in the privately held company he worked for. He can spread his lifetime capital gains exemption over the span of 5 years by selling 200,000 in shares per year.
