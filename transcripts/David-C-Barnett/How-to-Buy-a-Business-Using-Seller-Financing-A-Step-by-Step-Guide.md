The last question today is what are the steps to successfully buy a business with seller financing. Seller financing, everybody wants it, it's very sexy right now, and the great thing is that most business acquisition transactions have a component of seller financing. So my theory is the best way to do it is: most sellers want at least some money now, and if you are trying to be creative on giving them some money now but putting not a big down payment down, even if they were seller financing, I would propose SBA financing with a seller note as well. So what does that mean? That means if you want to put down as little as possible, let's just say it was a million dollars of total cost.

The bank can lend up to 900,000, assuming that the cash flow is strong from the tax returns to the business. You need 100 grand seller carry-back on full standby, which means no payments for the first two years, it can accrue interest. 75% so 75 grand. So I get 900 from the bank, I get 75,000 left in equity from the seller which accrues interest but no payments made. Now I need 25 grand.

Well, I don't want to put any money down, so I'm going to ask Bill to be a 5% owner of the business. He doesn't have to be a guarantor, he owns 5%, he gives me that 25 grand, he owns five, and then I now have a little bit of reserves to show the bank that I have post-closing liquidity. Boom, close a deal, none of my own money out of the pocket. So that I think is the better structure. Now if you can get a seller that's going to give you amazing financing terms, then all power to you, but the majority of the deals I see they want some money now.

So the SBA seller-finance deal with seller equity left in the deal on standby is the way to structure most of these deals. Not all of them, and it depends. Some deals don't cash flow, and so they don't report all their income, so they can't get SBA financing because there's no cash flow. So on deals like that, yeah, they're going to finance it because they're not going to get any other offers unless they get cash offers, and they're probably not going to. But anyways, at the end of the day, on a business acquisition with SBA financing the business still needs a third-party valuation, so it still needs to appraise for whatever the purchase price is.

Hope that helps.
