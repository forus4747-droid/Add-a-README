My name is Jonas Olsson, CEO and founder of Pest Badger. We went from zero to a nine-figure business with 14 locations in less than 5 years. For this video I'm going to assume you already have a business license, insurance, you're certified to do pest control, and you have the equipment to service your customers. Your first $300,000 in revenue is basically going to be done all bootstrapped and by yourself, unless you have a lot of cash from some other investment. What you have to know first is your average contract value.

Let's say it's between $150 to $250 per service, which is once a quarter. Doesn't matter if you bill monthly or bi-monthly. Let's go with average contract values per year between $600 and $800. That means you're going to need to get 400 to 500 clients that are all paying you that $600 to $800 per year to get that $300,000 in revenue. Because you're under a million dollars in revenue, really no one knows who you are.

Your job is to get as many clients in the door as fast as possible. The big challenge at this level is that you need to get a lot of clients in the door but you don't have a lot of money. So everything I'm teaching you is literally going to cost you zero dollars. The first thing I'd do is go on Facebook and find all the local Facebook groups. Remember on Facebook people are typically looking for a deal, they don't see a lot of value right away.

So make sure you're creating a lot of value-driven content, not just posting your business. What I mean by giving lots of value is showing them how-to videos — so if they want to go do it themselves and not hire you, they could treat it themselves if they wanted to. Get in all your local Facebook groups, find 10, 20, 30 depending on the market. The mom groups do very well. Start posting content a couple times a week just to get your name out there.

Posting on Facebook is all free. You're not going to run ads. You're not going to boost your post — that does nothing. Don't go hire a team, don't have a videographer. My most viral videos have all just been using an iPhone.

Before you even get a website, get a Google My Business. Is a website important? Yes, you can build a cheap website. But a Google My Business these days is way more important. When people search "pest control near me", the first people who have Google My Business are going to show up.

You'll have your phone number on there, website if you have one, pictures, and this is a place people can leave reviews. Next thing with Google My Business, also free, is get a Google Guarantee. It takes a little bit of time and effort to set up — you have to take pictures of your trucks, show your business license, make sure you have insurance, all the documentation. What happens is when people search pest control companies near me, it's going to show up at the very top with a little green check mark that says guaranteed. That simply means Google will track calls that come into your office, it will record the call for you, and if for some reason the customer has a bad experience, Google will actually pay that customer their money back.

Next I'd start knocking doors in the neighborhoods that you want to service. I'm just going to go knock doors and tell people what I do for a service. I'm not going to be salesy. I'm just going to say "Hey, I'm just in the area, this is what I'm doing and this is how I can help you." And offer them a free service in exchange for a five-star review. Yes, you're going to sell them the entire program, but those first 50 reviews we have to get as fast as possible onto Google My Business so that we can rank against the other pest control companies in our area.

The types of neighborhoods I'm going to look for: obviously high income earning or upper middle class, the ones who have disposable income to afford your services, and they typically have issues and they don't have the time. Two is proximity to my other clients or where the office is, so I can really start building out route density so I'm not traveling all over God's green earth trying to service these customers. Now we're going to offer each one of those customers a referral bonus. I'm not going to credit their account — I'm actually going to give them $50 for every single person that they give us as a referral that signs up. What makes referrals so valuable is that they trust their neighbors and friends and family who had a really good experience from your company.

I know that I'm going to close roughly 80% of those. So if they're giving me three to five referrals, I'm going to get at least two people per customer. So I know I'm going to go from 50 customers to 150 customers by those first 50 clients that I got. And referrals don't have to stop there, because when you get those next 100, that same 100 is going to give you three to five referrals as well. Next I would find a strategic partner or multiple strategic partners in my local area.

Think of other service-based companies that have the same avatar as you. We already said it's higher income, upper middle class with disposable income. So I'm going to look for lawn care companies that don't do pest control, HVAC, plumbing — any service-related company works really well. I'm going to sit down with the owner, and not only am I going to talk about referral strategy, but I'm going to figure out a way to get his customer list and give him my customer list to upsell both lists so that each wins. Let's say you go into a medium-sized company and they've got a base of 1,000 clients.

If you can get 10% of their customer list, that's 100 quotes sent out, and you're going to close 50% of those — you're going to get 50 customers just by making a strategic partnership like that. And lastly, become a local celebrity, which just means posting free content. Post on all social media platforms — LinkedIn, YouTube, TikTok, Facebook, Instagram. Post religiously, once a day. If you can't do that, start out small: once a week, then twice a week, then three times a week until you get to daily.

Make sure everyone in your area knows exactly what you do, so anytime anyone has an issue they're coming to you. When other people have issues they're going to tag you when someone posts on Next Door or Facebook. Last year, using this strategy alone, we created over 100 million views, which generated hundreds of thousands of dollars in sales.
