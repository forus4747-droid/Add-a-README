You asked for a proven system before you build anything. Here is what the actual data says — including the parts that kill three of the four models you were offered, and the part that undercuts my own strongest evidence.

What "proven" can honestly mean

Three different claims, only one of which is provable

"Proven system" gets used to mean three separate things, and the difference is the whole game:

  • This model has produced real revenue for real people. Provable. Public revenue dashboards, platform payout data, tax filings. I have this below.
  • This model produces revenue for most people who try it. Also provable — and for most "online business" models, the answer is emphatically no. I have this below too, and it is the more useful half.
  • This model will produce revenue for you. Not provable by any document, mine included. Only a test can answer it.

Anyone selling you the first claim while implying the third is running a con. So this document gives you the first two honestly, then hands you the cheapest possible version of the third.

Digital product / template shop

Ruled out

A median outcome of $72/month is not a system, it's a raffle ticket. The top-1% concentration tells you what actually drives sales here: an audience you already have. The product is the monetisation layer, not the business. With no audience and a need for income, this is the wrong end of the pipe — it is something to add in year two, not to start with.

Finding 02

AI already ate the job you were probably going to sell

This is the finding most "use AI to make money" advice omits, and it comes from the strongest source in this document — a peer-reviewed economics study, not a blog.

Read the second-order finding carefully, because it inverts the usual advice: the highest-rated, best-paid freelancers were hurt the most. Being good at the commodity task offered no protection. AI did not create a level playing field for newcomers in these categories — it flooded them.

The trap this sets

The instinct when you have AI and no other skill is to sell AI-produced writing, design, or video cheaply. That is precisely the segment the data shows collapsing, and you would be entering it with no reputation, at the bottom of a price war, against everyone else who had the same instinct. Whatever you build, it cannot be this.

Those last two numbers together are the whole strategy. The product works for the people who buy it — but almost nobody is buying it framed as "AI," and the ones who do spend under $100 a month. Meanwhile the generalist AI-automation-agency tier is described by practitioners as saturated, precisely because it's the path every course teaches: cold-emailing tech-forward startups who already get ten identical pitches a week.

"AI automation agency"

Only if inverted

Do not sell AI. Sell a named business outcome at a fixed price — more booked jobs, a month of published content, a backlog of unanswered reviews cleared — to a buyer who neither knows nor cares that AI produced it. AI is your cost structure, not your product. That reframing is the difference between competing with every course graduate and competing with the local agency charging ten times your price.

The part that matters most

Where all of this evidence is weak

Survivorship bias — read this before you trust the table above

Every public revenue dashboard in Finding 05 is a survivor. Nobody publishes a dashboard for the productized service that made $0 and got quietly shut down after four months. I cannot tell you the denominator — how many people ran that exact play and got nothing — because that number is not collected by anyone. This is the single biggest weakness in this document, and it applies to essentially all "proof" of this kind that you will ever be shown, including from people charging you for it.

Two further caveats you're owed:

  • I could not open the primary documents. This environment's network blocked direct page fetches, so every figure here except the academic study comes from search-result summaries of those sources. The confidence tags are honest: STRONG means peer-reviewed or official, MODERATE means a credible report I could not open myself, WEAK means a single unverifiable secondary source. Re-check anything tagged WEAK at the linked source before you bet on it.
  • Base rates are not your rate. Population statistics describe the average attempt, which includes everyone who quit in week two. They bound your expectations; they don't determine your outcome.
The actual proof of concept

A falsifiable test, under $100, answer in three weeks

No document can prove this works for you. What it can do is design the cheapest experiment that produces a real yes or no. This is that experiment. It is numbered because the order genuinely matters — each step is only valid if the one before it held.

  1. Pick one narrow niche of offline businesses

    Not "small businesses." Something like independent dental practices in one metro area, HVAC contractors, boutique law firms, med spas, driving schools. Three requirements: they already spend money on marketing, you can find 200+ of them with names and emails, and they are not tech companies.

    WHY → Reply rates run 5× higher outside software · saturation is concentrated in tech-forward buyers

  2. Pick one outcome you can deliver in under four hours

    Fixed scope, fixed price, visible result. Examples that fit the growth categories in Finding 03: a month of short-form vertical video cut from footage they already have; their Google Business Profile rewritten plus 30 backlogged review replies drafted; a 10-page location-targeted content pack. Never describe it as AI. Describe the deliverable and the date.

    WHY → Finding 04: they buy outcomes, not AI · Finding 02: never sell the commodity output itself

  3. Do the work first, unpaid, for ten of them

    Build the list by hand from Google Maps. Then actually produce the deliverable for ten named businesses before contacting them, and send it finished — not a pitch, the thing itself, with a note offering the same monthly at a fixed price. This is the one move only you can afford: your marginal cost of production is near zero, and theirs isn't.

    WHY → Small lists reply at 5.8% vs 2.1% · a finished deliverable is not a cold pitch, it's a demonstration

  4. Then send 90 more, in batches of 20

    Same niche, same offer, personalised opening line referencing something specific. One follow-up each, four days later. Change nothing else until all 100 are sent — you are measuring one offer against one market, and two variables at once tells you nothing.

    EXPECT → 3–6 replies per 100 at benchmark · under 2 means the list or offer is wrong, not the model

  5. Price the first three clients at $300–750 fixed

    Never hourly — hourly caps you at your speed and gives away the entire AI advantage. Fixed price means a four-hour delivery at $500 is a $125/hour business. Take payment through a Stripe payment link before you start work.

    WHY → Finding 05: fixed scope is the mechanism that makes the margin · $199–549/mo is the documented band

  6. Apply the kill criteria without negotiating with yourself

    These are decided now, in advance, precisely so you cannot rationalise later. Write them down before you send email one.

    100 SENT, <2 REPLIES → change the offer, keep the niche
    200 SENT, 0 CALLS BOOKED → change the niche, keep the offer
    300 SENT, 0 PAID → kill this offer entirely, restart at step 2
    3 OFFERS × 300 SENT, 0 PAID → cold email is not your channel; the model is not disproven

Two things that will produce a false negative

Deliverability. A brand-new domain sending cold email gets spam-foldered. Warm it up for 10–14 days with normal low-volume sending before the test, or you'll measure your spam score rather than your offer.

Legality. Unsolicited B2B email is lawful in the US under CAN-SPAM if you include a real physical address and a working opt-out, and never use deceptive subject lines. The UK and EU are considerably stricter under GDPR/PECR. Check the rules for wherever your prospects are before sending, not after.

Bottom line

What the evidence actually supports

Four constraints, jointly, survive everything above: sell a service, not a product (median product outcome is $72/month). Sell to businesses, not consumers (they have budgets and a reason to spend them). Sell an outcome, never "AI" (82% never pay for AI as such; the ones who buy results are satisfied). Sell to boring offline niches, not tech (5× the reply rate, and that's where the saturation isn't).

What no evidence supports is anything passive, anything without outreach, or anything that skips the part where you talk to strangers who don't want to hear from you. Every documented winner in this file has a distribution channel they worked. That is the actual system — and it's the part the people selling you systems leave out.

Run the $39 test. It will tell you more in three weeks than any further reading will.
